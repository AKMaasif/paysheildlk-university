# Evaluation results

Generated by `python -m evaluation.run` (seed 7, 192 cases, 213.9 s). Model: **mock (deterministic)**.

**Read this first.** All data is synthetic. A scenario's fraud label comes from how the scenario was built, not from the rules, so the rules can be wrong and the harness counts it. These numbers show how this design behaves on invented cases. They say nothing about real customers or real fraud rates. With no model key, every model-written note is the deterministic mock, so notes are checked for plumbing and privacy, not for quality.

## 3. Single-agent baseline

One component that reads every feed, holds every tool and decides alone, using the same rule table.

|  | Accuracy | Recall | False-hold rate | p50 latency | p95 latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-agent graph | 85.9% | 95.4% | 26.2% | 246 ms | 453 ms |
| Single agent (rules) | 85.9% | 95.4% | 26.2% | 8 ms | 11 ms |

Same decision as the multi-agent graph: 192 of 192. Accuracy is the same because the decision comes from the same rules. The multi-agent graph is slower: it signs every handoff, writes events and keeps a record. That cost buys the separation below, not accuracy.

Model-only single agent: **not run.** No META_API_KEY or XAI_API_KEY is set. The mock model has no judgement, so a result from it would be invented.

## 4. What the customer profile changed

The same scenarios decided twice: once from the signals alone, once with the customer's own history (usual payees and amounts) taken into account. The profile only moves the score. It never touches the block-at-once rule or the swap-plus-warning-sign rule.

|  | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | False-hold rate | Missed-fraud rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signals only | 65.6% | 70.6% | 66.7% | 35.7% | 33.3% |
| With customer profile | 85.9% | 82.4% | 95.4% | 26.2% | 4.6% |

Scenario types whose flag rate moved:

| Scenario | Truth | Flagged before | Flagged after |
|---|---|---|---|
| legit_new_phone | legitimate | 16.7% | 0.0% |
| legit_rent_after_new_phone | legitimate | 100.0% | 50.0% |
| split_payment | fraud | 0.0% | 100.0% |
| stale_swap | fraud | 0.0% | 100.0% |
| sub_threshold | fraud | 0.0% | 58.3% |


What moved: recall changed by +28.7 points and the false-hold rate by -9.5 points. The profile lowers the score for an established payee and a usual amount, and raises it for an amount more than twice the customer's usual high shortly after a SIM change. The raise needs at least five earlier payments, so it stays quiet for customers with a short ledger. It does nothing for fraud that stays inside a customer's usual pattern. The histories here are invented, so the size of the change is not a prediction.

## 5. The feedback loop, with a person in it

Customer answers to held payments are the only feedback signal. In this harness the answers are **simulated from the scenario labels (fraud answers No, legitimate answers Yes and passes the questions)**, so this checks the mechanism, not the world.


| Score band | Answered holds | Fraud | False holds | Hold was right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 45 to 54 | 43 | 31 | 12 | 72.1% |
| 55 to 64 | 13 | 12 | 1 | 92.3% |
| 65 to 74 | 39 | 31 | 8 | 79.5% |
| 75 to 84 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 100.0% |
| 85 to 94 | 12 | 8 | 4 | 66.7% |
| 95 to 100 | 10 | 10 | 0 | 100.0% |

The Analytics agent made **no proposal**: it needs 20 answered holds in the lowest band and no confirmed fraud in it. That is the guard working.


Why the gate matters. What raising the hold score would have done on the same scenarios:


| Hold score | Recall | False-hold rate | Missed-fraud rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45 | 95.4% | 26.2% | 4.6% |
| 55 | 66.7% | 14.3% | 33.3% |
| 65 | 55.6% | 14.3% | 44.4% |

A higher score means fewer false holds and more missed fraud. The system cannot see missed fraud on its own, so a person decides.

## 6. Hostile text in the payee field

30 runs: 15 payloads against a routine payment and the same against a takeover. Decision unchanged, nothing outside a charter, no raw identifiers sent to the model: **30 of 30**.


With the mock model nothing here can actually be talked into anything, because the mock ignores the text. What this checks is the code around the model: the decision does not depend on it, the privacy guard holds, and unusual text does not crash a step. Run it again with a real model key to test the model itself.

## 7. The same lookups through MCP

- Same decision, score and ledger result: **16 of 16**.

- Median time per case: 326 ms direct, 351 ms through MCP. MCP calls made: 120. Fallbacks to a direct call: 0.

MCP adds a standard way in and a second charter check. It does not change any outcome, and it costs time.

## 8. Limits

- Synthetic data and invented fraud patterns. The rules were written by the same team that wrote the red-team scenarios.
- No real fraud labels, so precision and recall describe the scenario mix, which we chose.
- The mock model is the default. Model-dependent behaviour is not measured unless a key is set.
- The selfie check compares two frames. It is not face recognition, and its numbers are for plumbing only.
- Latency is measured in-process on one machine with no step delay and no network.
